About the apartment

Elegance and comfort in the heart of Subotica

Apartman Mirakul is located in the iconic Prokeš Palace — one of the most beautiful buildings in the center of Subotica, dating back to the era when Art Nouveau shaped the city's unique character. Whether you are visiting for a weekend getaway, a business trip, or simply to explore Subotica from its finest location — Mirakul is where comfort, atmosphere, and the city come together.

Prokeš Palace — heart of the Korzo since 1887

Prokeš Palace was built in 1887, designed by local architect Géza Koczka. It spans three streets — the Korzo promenade, Engelsova Street and Đure Đakovića Street — and was the largest rental building in Subotica at the time of its construction.

Built in the spirit of Italian Renaissance, with a massive ground floor, rusticated facade treatment and arched portals, the palace remains one of the most recognizable buildings in central Subotica. As the natural endpoint of the Korzo, it has shaped the look and identity of the city's core for over a century.

Apartman Mirakul is located right inside Prokeš Palace, at the very heart of Subotica. City Hall, the Synagogue, Rajhl Palace and other landmarks are just a few minutes' walk away — your stay begins inside one of the most significant historic buildings in the city.

Subotica and the Railway — a City That Grew Along the Rails

With the arrival of the railway in the second half of the 19th century, Subotica grew from a Pannonian market town into one of the largest and wealthiest cities in the then Kingdom of Hungary. The railway brought trade, industry, and new residents, setting in motion a period of prosperity that permanently shaped the city's appearance.

It was this prosperity that gave rise to many of the landmarks for which Subotica is known today — the City Hall, the Synagogue, the Art Nouveau palaces, and the grand buildings along the Korzo, including Prokeš Palace. As an important railway hub, Subotica became a meeting point of Central Europe's commercial and cultural routes.

The city remains excellently connected by rail today. A high-speed railway links Subotica with Novi Sad and Belgrade, daily services connect it with Szeged, and the completion of the high-speed line towards Budapest is eagerly anticipated. Apartman Mirakul is just a few minutes' walk from Subotica Railway Station, meaning that for many guests, their stay begins almost the moment they step off the train.
